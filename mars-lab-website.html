Research

The lab's work sits at the intersection of radiating structures and the materials that surround them — spanning conventional printed antennas, engineered leaky-wave apertures, and the measurement infrastructure needed to characterize both.

Material characterization and near-field measurement feed antenna design across patch and leaky-wave elements, which in turn extend into sub-THz communication links.

Patch Antennas

Compact printed radiators for integrated RF front-ends, optimized for bandwidth, polarization purity, and gain within tight footprint constraints.

Leaky-Wave Antennas

Traveling-wave apertures that radiate continuously along their length, enabling frequency-scanned and beam-shaped patterns from a single structure.

Material Characterization

Free-space and guided-wave methods for extracting the permittivity and permeability of conventional and engineered materials across microwave and mm-wave bands.

Near-Field Chamber

Controlled measurement of radiating structures at close range, with near-to-far-field transformation used to validate simulated antenna performance.

THz Communications

Antenna and passive-component design for sub-terahertz links, where propagation loss and fabrication tolerance drive every design decision.
